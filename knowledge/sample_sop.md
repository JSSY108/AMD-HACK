# Standard Operating Procedures — Industrial Inspection
# Task 16: Enhanced with description, risk, consequence, action, escalation

## SOP-001: Surface Crack Detection and Response

### Description
Surface cracks are visible linear discontinuities on structural components, pressure vessels, piping, and load-bearing equipment. They may appear as hairline fractures or wider fissures, often found near weld joints, stress concentration points, or heat-affected zones.

### Risk
**HIGH** — Surface cracks can propagate under cyclic loading and lead to catastrophic structural failure. Crack growth rate accelerates under vibration, thermal cycling, and sustained pressure.

### Consequence
- Crack propagation under operational loads leading to component fracture
- Catastrophic failure during service causing equipment destruction
- Potential safety hazard to nearby personnel (flying debris, pressure release)
- Unplanned downtime costing thousands per hour

### Identification Criteria
- Visible linear discontinuity on the surface
- May appear as hairline fractures or wider fissures
- Often found near weld joints, stress concentration points, or heat-affected zones
- May show discoloration or oxidation along crack edges

### Immediate Actions
1. Mark the crack location with paint or tape
2. Measure crack length and width if safe to do so
3. Photograph the defect from multiple angles with scale reference
4. Report to shift supervisor immediately
5. Establish exclusion zone if crack is on pressure-containing equipment

### Recommended Response
- **Severity LOW (hairline, <2mm):** Schedule inspection within 7 days. Monitor for growth.
- **Severity MEDIUM (2-10mm):** Reduce operational load by 50%. Inspect within 48 hours. Document growth rate.
- **Severity HIGH (>10mm or near critical joint):** Immediate shutdown of affected system. Do not operate until engineering assessment is complete.

### Escalation Condition
- Crack length increases between observations → **IMMEDIATE SHUTDOWN**
- Crack appears near high-pressure or high-temperature zone → **EMERGENCY PROTOCOL**
- Multiple cracks detected in same component → **Engineering review within 4 hours**
- Audible cracking sounds reported → **Evacuate area, emergency shutdown**


## SOP-002: Corrosion and Rust Management

### Description
Surface corrosion includes uniform rust, pitting corrosion, and galvanic corrosion on metallic components. Manifests as discoloration (orange, brown, white deposits), surface roughness, pitting, or flaking of protective coatings.

### Risk
**MEDIUM to HIGH** — Corrosion progressively degrades material thickness and structural integrity. Pitting corrosion is particularly dangerous as it creates stress concentration points.

### Consequence
- Progressive material loss reducing load-bearing capacity
- Perforation of pressure boundaries leading to leaks
- Contamination of process fluids affecting product quality
- Accelerated failure when combined with stress (stress corrosion cracking)

### Identification Criteria
- Discoloration: orange/brown (iron oxide), white (zinc/aluminum oxide)
- Surface roughness, pitting, or cratering
- Flaking or delamination of protective coatings
- Weeping or staining from concealed corrosion

### Immediate Actions
1. Document extent, location, and depth of corrosion with photographs
2. Measure remaining wall thickness if accessible (UT gauge)
3. Assess proximity to load-bearing or pressure-containing areas
4. Check if corrosion inhibitor systems are functioning

### Recommended Response
- **Surface rust only:** Clean and apply protective coating. Re-inspect in 30 days.
- **Pitting corrosion:** Measure pit depth. If >25% wall thickness loss, flag for engineering review.
- **Active corrosion near welds:** Immediate engineering assessment required.
- **Corrosion under insulation (CUI):** Remove insulation for full inspection within 1 week.

### Escalation Condition
- Wall thickness below minimum design threshold → **IMMEDIATE SHUTDOWN**
- Corrosion rate exceeds 0.5mm/year → **Increase inspection frequency to monthly**
- Active leaking through corroded area → **Emergency isolation and repair**
- Corrosion progressing between inspections → **Engineering review and re-coating**


## SOP-003: Missing or Loose Fasteners

### Description
Covers all bolted connections including flanges, structural joints, equipment mounts, safety guards, and access panels. Missing or loose fasteners compromise joint integrity.

### Risk
**MEDIUM** — Missing fasteners reduce clamping force and can lead to joint failure under vibration, thermal expansion, or operational loads.

### Consequence
- Joint failure under operational loads or vibration
- Equipment detachment or separation during operation
- Safety guard failure exposing personnel to moving parts
- Secondary damage to adjacent components from loose hardware

### Identification Criteria
- Visible empty bolt holes
- Fasteners protruding abnormally or at wrong angle
- Loose or finger-tight connections (verified by touch)
- Missing lock washers, split pins, or safety wire
- Evidence of bolt shearing (broken bolt heads)

### Immediate Actions
1. Count missing/loose fasteners and document locations
2. Assess the criticality of the joint (safety-critical vs. non-critical)
3. Prevent equipment operation if safety-critical fasteners are missing
4. Check torque on adjacent fasteners (they may also be loose)

### Recommended Response
- **Non-critical location (1-2 fasteners):** Replace at next scheduled maintenance window.
- **Critical joint or safety guard:** Immediate replacement required before resuming operation.
- **Pattern of loosening:** Investigate root cause (vibration, thermal cycling, improper torque).

### Escalation Condition
- Safety guard fasteners missing → **LOCKOUT/TAGOUT immediately**
- More than 25% of fasteners in a joint are missing → **Shutdown affected system**
- Fasteners loosening repeatedly after retorque → **Engineering investigation required**


## SOP-004: Misalignment and Deformation

### Description
Visible misalignment of rotating equipment, structural members, piping supports, and mechanical assemblies. Includes bent, bowed, or warped components.

### Risk
**MEDIUM to HIGH** — Misalignment causes accelerated wear on bearings and seals, excessive vibration, increased energy consumption, and potential catastrophic failure.

### Consequence
- Premature bearing failure (lifespan reduced by 50-90%)
- Seal damage causing fluid leaks
- Increased energy consumption (up to 15% power loss)
- Fatigue cracking at stress concentration points
- Coupling failure and shaft damage

### Identification Criteria
- Visible offset between mating surfaces or shafts
- Uneven gaps in flanged connections
- Bent or bowed structural members
- Shaft runout visible to the eye
- Unusual vibration or noise during operation

### Immediate Actions
1. Document the misalignment with photographs showing offset
2. Measure offset using straight edge, dial indicator, or laser alignment tool
3. Check for secondary damage (bearing temperature, seal leaks)
4. Record vibration levels if monitoring equipment available

### Recommended Response
- **Minor misalignment (<1mm):** Schedule correction at next maintenance window.
- **Moderate misalignment (1-5mm):** Reduce speed/load. Correct within 1 week.
- **Severe misalignment (>5mm):** Shutdown and realign before operation.

### Escalation Condition
- Bearing temperature exceeding normal range → **Immediate shutdown**
- Visible shaft deflection during operation → **Emergency stop**
- Progressive misalignment increasing between checks → **Root cause investigation**


## SOP-005: Safety Equipment Compliance

### Description
Inspection of safety barriers, emergency exits, fire suppression equipment, PPE stations, warning signage, and machine guarding. Non-compliance directly endangers personnel.

### Risk
**CRITICAL** — Safety equipment deficiencies directly increase injury risk and regulatory liability. Any gap must be treated as urgent.

### Consequence
- Increased risk of personnel injury or fatality
- Regulatory violations leading to fines and operational suspension
- Legal liability in case of incident
- Loss of safety certification or operating permits

### Identification Criteria
- Missing, damaged, or displaced safety guards on moving equipment
- Blocked or obstructed emergency exits or access routes
- Expired, discharged, or missing fire extinguishers
- Damaged, faded, or missing warning signs and safety labels
- Empty or disorganized PPE stations
- Disabled safety interlocks or bypassed safety systems

### Immediate Actions
1. Immediately report to safety officer / shift supervisor
2. Restrict access to affected area if active hazard exists
3. Deploy temporary safety measures (barriers, cones, signage)
4. Document deficiency with photographs and location details

### Recommended Response
- **Any safety equipment deficiency:** Treat as CRITICAL. Immediate remediation required.
- **Missing guards on moving equipment:** Immediate lockout/tagout until replaced.
- **Expired safety equipment:** Replace within 24 hours maximum.
- **Blocked emergency exit:** Clear immediately — zero tolerance.

### Escalation Condition
- ANY bypassed safety interlock → **IMMEDIATE SHUTDOWN, management notification**
- Missing machine guarding on operating equipment → **Emergency stop**
- Fire suppression system offline → **Notify fire safety team, implement fire watch**


## SOP-006: Fluid Leaks and Spills

### Description
Visible fluid leakage including hydraulic fluid, lubricants, coolants, process chemicals, and water. Can create safety hazards, environmental contamination, and equipment damage.

### Risk
**MEDIUM to CRITICAL** — Risk level depends on fluid type, volume, and location. Flammable fluids near heat sources are critical. Any spill on walking surfaces creates slip hazard.

### Consequence
- Slip and fall injuries to personnel
- Environmental contamination and regulatory penalties
- Equipment damage from loss of lubrication or cooling
- Fire or explosion risk with flammable fluids
- Product contamination in process areas

### Identification Criteria
- Wet spots, puddles, or drip marks on equipment or floor
- Staining or discoloration on equipment surfaces
- Visible dripping from connections, seals, or fittings
- Oily residue on floors, walkways, or equipment
- Fluid level dropping in reservoirs without explanation

### Immediate Actions
1. Identify the fluid type if possible (color, odor, location)
2. Contain the spill using appropriate absorbent materials
3. Mark the area as a slip hazard with warning signs/cones
4. Report to maintenance and safety simultaneously
5. Do NOT attempt cleanup of unknown chemicals without PPE

### Recommended Response
- **Water/coolant:** Clean and repair source. Monitor for recurrence.
- **Hydraulic/lubricant:** Immediate source repair. Environmental containment required.
- **Chemical/hazardous:** Evacuate area. HazMat response team required.
- **Fuel/flammable:** Eliminate ignition sources. Emergency response protocol.

### Escalation Condition
- Leak rate increasing over time → **Immediate isolation**
- Any leak of hazardous/toxic material → **EVACUATION, HazMat response**
- Leak near electrical equipment → **De-energize and isolate**
- Flammable fluid near heat source → **Emergency shutdown and fire watch**


## SOP-007: Vibration and Noise Anomalies

### Description
Unusual vibration patterns or abnormal noise from rotating equipment, bearings, gearboxes, motors, or structural components indicating potential mechanical failure.

### Risk
**MEDIUM to HIGH** — Abnormal vibration is often a precursor to catastrophic bearing, shaft, or structural failure. Early detection prevents costly unplanned downtime.

### Consequence
- Bearing seizure causing shaft damage
- Gear tooth failure and gearbox destruction
- Foundation bolt loosening from excessive vibration
- Structural fatigue cracking
- Complete machine failure and extended downtime

### Identification Criteria
- Audible changes in operating sound (grinding, knocking, whining)
- Visible vibration of machine frame or foundation
- Loose covers, panels, or guards rattling
- Increased temperature at bearing locations
- Unusual patterns in vibration monitoring data

### Immediate Actions
1. Note the type of noise/vibration (continuous, intermittent, load-dependent)
2. Check bearing temperatures with infrared thermometer
3. Verify lubrication levels and condition
4. Document with audio/video recording if possible

### Recommended Response
- **Slight increase in normal vibration:** Monitor. Schedule analysis within 1 week.
- **New unusual noise:** Reduce load. Inspect within 24 hours.
- **Severe vibration or grinding noise:** Immediate shutdown for inspection.

### Escalation Condition
- Vibration levels doubling from baseline → **Shutdown within 4 hours**
- Metal-on-metal grinding sound → **IMMEDIATE SHUTDOWN**
- Bearing temperature > 80°C (or 20°C above normal) → **Shutdown and replace**
